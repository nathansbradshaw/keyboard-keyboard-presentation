These are the actual bodges from this project. A bodge wire is a controlled PCB revision, not an admission of defeat. Begin unpowered. Identify the two electrical endpoints from the schematic and PCB layout, then confirm them with continuity mode. If an existing trace connects something it should not, cut it with a sharp knife and magnification. Make one shallow cut, inspect it, and prove the connection is open before adding anything. Use an exposed pad or via as the new attachment point whenever possible. If there is no accessible copper, gently scrape a few millimeters of solder mask, add flux, and tin the copper without lingering long enough to delaminate it. Pre-tin a short piece of insulated 30 AWG wire, solder each end, then anchor the wire so movement cannot pull on the pads. Kapton is good for temporary restraint; UV-curable solder mask or electronics-safe adhesive can become the final strain relief after the electrical repair is proven. Check the intended continuity and adjacent shorts with power off. First power-up should use a current-limited supply. Never use continuity or resistance mode on a powered board. Transition: sometimes the copper is correct and the component is what has to leave.

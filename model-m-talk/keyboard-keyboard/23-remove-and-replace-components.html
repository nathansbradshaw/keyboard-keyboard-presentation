More flux and patience cost less than a ripped pad or via.

These photos show the actual U1 removal and replacement. Choose the removal method by package, thermal mass, and whether the part needs to survive. For a two-terminal resistor, capacitor, or diode, add flux and a little fresh solder; the new flux helps the old joint flow. Heat both ends nearly together with two irons, hot tweezers, or a broad solder blob, then lift with almost no force. For an IC, protect nearby plastics with Kapton or a shield, use low airflow so tiny parts do not fly away, and move heat evenly around the package. Lift only when every joint is visibly molten. Through-hole connectors are heat sinks. If the inexpensive component is already faulty, cut its body or leads and desolder one pin at a time rather than trying to save it at the board's expense. A low-melting-point removal alloy can help on stubborn multi-pin packages. After removal, add flux, wick only until the pads are flat, clean the residue as appropriate for the flux, and inspect for lifted pads or solder bridges under magnification. Work with ventilation, observe ESD precautions, disconnect power, and discharge large capacitors. The key rule is tactile: if the component resists, stop pulling. Transition: with clean pads, installing a replacement is the easy case—but the board can also accept a component the schematic never planned for.

設計図から実物へ

The schematic says what connectsPCB layout decides how it exists in the real world.

The PCB editor is where the logical circuit becomes a physical object. First, define the board outline: its shape, dimensions, mounting features, and any areas where components or copper cannot go. Next, place the physical components where they will be soldered, grouping related parts, leaving room for connectors, and keeping sensitive analog paths short. Then route the required connections as actual copper traces across the front and back layers, using vias where a path needs to change layers. This board carries one hundred MX switch footprints, one hundred Hall-sensor footprints, fifteen analog-mux packages, and twelve potentiometers across 447.04 by 162.81 millimeters of two-layer FR-4. Finally, run the design-rule checks and inspect the layout for shorts, inadequate clearances, unrouted connections, poor return paths, and traces close enough to create crosstalk or other signal-integrity problems. Transition: once those checks are clean, the board is ready to leave KiCad entirely.

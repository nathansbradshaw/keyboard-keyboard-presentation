The company is not the license. Verify every design you reuse. General information—not legal advice.

Open hardware is not merely a schematic you can look at. OSHWA defines it as design source people may study, modify, distribute, make, and sell. That is why copying can be an intended feature—but the exact license is the permission slip. SparkFun says its breakout-board schematics and designs are open on GitHub. Adafruit publishes design files for Adafruit-designed PCBs. Arduino explains that most derived official board designs must publish CAD and BOM under CC BY-SA 4.0. Great Scott Gadgets says everything it produces is open source. Olimex has open families that expressly permit personal and commercial reuse, while other Olimex designs are proprietary—proof that the product, revision, and repo matter more than the company name. Reveal the skeptical reaction on “read the license,” then preserve attribution and notices, comply with share-alike or source obligations, and never reuse a trademark or imply endorsement.

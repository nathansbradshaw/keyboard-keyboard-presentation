まず小さく証明 A breadboard is cheaper than a board revision.
This is the actual keyboard-keyboard proof of concept. Before paying for a full-size PCB, I put five keys, Hall sensors, the controller, sensor boards, and test equipment on breadboards. The goal was not to make a pretty mini keyboard. It was to retire the riskiest assumption: could the magnet and Hall sensor produce a repeatable signal across real key travel, could the controller read it, and could that motion become a MIDI note? Build the smallest end-to-end slice, then measure voltage range, noise, travel, timing, and repeatability. A POC does not prove the full board. It will not reveal every routing, grounding, power-distribution, manufacturing, or hundred-key scaling problem—we later found those too. It proves enough of the sensing chain to justify the next investment. If one key cannot work on a breadboard, one hundred copies on a PCB will only make the mistake more expensive.

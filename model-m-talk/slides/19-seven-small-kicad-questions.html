This is the mental map to keep after the three KiCad walkthroughs. The application looks enormous, but the work is a loop of seven ordinary questions: what connects, what each part is called, what package fits, where it lives, where the copper goes, whether the rules still pass, and what the fabricator will actually receive. The project files are structured text, so put the whole project—including custom symbols and footprints—in Git and make known-good checkpoints. Do not save ERC and DRC for the end. For anything mechanical, print the board at one-to-one scale and place the real parts on the paper. Finally, inspect the plotted Gerbers rather than assuming the editor view is what the factory sees. The cheapest board revision is the one caught before checkout. Transition: the next set of habits starts one level lower, with choosing and proving the actual electronic parts.

測ってから直す Don’t guess. Change one thing, then measure again.
When the boards arrive, resist the urge to plug one in and hope. Start with the multimeter while the power is off: check continuity and look for shorts. Then power it and check every rail under load. If a digital bus is acting weird, the logic analyzer will show whether I²C acknowledged, chip select moved, or the UART sent anything. Use the scope when you need to see what the meter hides—noise, ripple, bad clocks, sensor waveforms, or a rail collapsing during activity. The iron lets you repair a joint, swap a part, cut a trace, or add a bodge wire. Make sure the board exposes its programming and debug pins too. STM32 often means ST-Link; J-Link and CMSIS-DAP are other common options. A current-limited bench supply, good flux, wick, tweezers, magnification, and hot air can come later. Whatever tools you have, change one thing at a time and measure again. Never use resistance or continuity mode on a powered board, and understand your scope’s grounding before you connect it.

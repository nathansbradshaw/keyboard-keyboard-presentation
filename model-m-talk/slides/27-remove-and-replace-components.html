These are photos of the U1 we removed and the replacement we soldered in. Let the “Omae wa mou… desoldered” joke land first. The real rule is simple: if the part does not lift easily, one of the joints is still holding it. For a resistor, capacitor, or diode, add flux and a little fresh solder, heat both ends, and lift gently. For an IC, shield nearby plastic, keep the hot-air flow low, and heat the package evenly until every joint is molten. A dead through-hole part is often not worth saving. Cut its body or leads and clear one hole at a time instead of risking the board. Low-melting-point removal alloy can help with stubborn packages. Once the part is off, use flux and wick to flatten the pads, clean the residue, and inspect for lifted pads or bridges under magnification. Disconnect power, discharge large capacitors, use ventilation, and follow ESD precautions. If the component resists, stop pulling. Transition: with clean pads, installing the replacement is the easy part—but the board can also take a component the schematic never planned for.
